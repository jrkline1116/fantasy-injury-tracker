Planning Around Bye Weeks in Fantasy Football

By John Kline · Updated October 16, 2026

Every NFL team gets one week off during the regular season. Byes are announced with the schedule in the spring, so unlike injuries they're completely predictable, and yet they cost fantasy managers points every season through forgotten lineup spots and last-minute waiver scrambles. A little planning removes the problem.

How bye weeks work

Byes are spread across the middle of the season, usually from around week 5 to week 14. Several teams are off in most of those weeks, and some weeks have many more teams off than others. The heavy weeks are when fantasy rosters get stretched, because several of your players can be out at once.

Find your problem weeks early

Go through your roster after the draft and write down each starter's bye. You're looking for two things:

  • Stacked byes: two or more starters at the same position off in the same week, such as both of your starting receivers.
  • Single points of failure: a position where you only roster one player, usually quarterback, tight end, kicker or defense. Each of those needs a plan for its bye week.

Don't panic about stacked byes on draft day. Taking one bad week for a stronger roster the rest of the season is usually a good trade, especially if that week falls early.

When to make the move

Most leagues run waivers on Tuesday night or Wednesday morning. The best time to pick up a bye-week replacement is the waiver run before the bye week, not the morning of the games, when only scraps are left. If you can look two weeks ahead, you can sometimes grab the replacement a week early, before other managers with the same problem are bidding against you.

Prefer replacements whose matchup that week is good, and who could stay useful later. A streaming defense or kicker with a favorable opponent is the classic example; our sister site Fantasy Defense Assist ranks every defense matchup for the rest of the season, which makes bye-week streaming easy to plan.

Byes and injuries together

The weeks that sink fantasy teams are usually a bye on top of an injury. When a starter gets hurt in a week when your backup at that position is on bye, you need a waiver pickup immediately. Keeping your bench balanced by position, rather than holding three backup tight ends, helps you absorb both at once.
