Game-Day Inactives: The 90-Minute Window That Decides Your Lineup

By John Kline · Updated October 16, 2026

The injury report tells you who might not play. The inactives list tells you who won't. Each team announces its inactive players about 90 minutes before kickoff, and it's the last official word before the game starts. If a questionable player isn't on it, he's playing.

Knowing when those lists come out, and setting up your lineup so you can still react, is one of the simplest edges in fantasy football.

When the lists come out

Roughly 90 minutes before each game. For a typical Sunday that means three waves:

  • Early games: inactives about an hour and a half before the early kickoff.
  • Late-afternoon games: inactives about an hour and a half before those kickoffs, by which time the early games are already being played.
  • Night games: Sunday night, Monday night and Thursday night lists come 90 minutes before those games.

International games kick off in the morning in U.S. time zones, so their inactives arrive very early. Check the kickoff time rather than assuming the usual windows.

What's on the list

Teams can't dress every player on the roster, so each game day a handful of healthy players are declared inactive along with the injured ones. For fantasy you only care whether your players are on it. A player listed out on Friday will be inactive; the useful information is about the questionable players.

Being active doesn't guarantee a normal role. A player returning from injury can be active but on a snap count, or active only as an emergency backup. Pre-game reports from beat writers often fill in that detail.

Set up your lineup before you need to

The inactives window only helps if you can still make a move when the news arrives. A few habits make that possible:

  1. Know your league's lock rule. Most leagues lock each player at his own game's kickoff, so you can change a late-game spot even after the early games start. A few leagues lock the whole lineup at the first game, which changes everything below.
  2. Keep the flex for the latest game. If a questionable player plays at 4:25, try to put him (or another late player) in your flex. If he's inactive, any bench player at any position can take that spot, as long as that player's game hasn't started.
  3. Have a backup in the same or a later window. A questionable 4:25 running back is easy to cover with a bench running back who also plays late. If your only backup played at 1:00, he's locked by then.
  4. Be reachable. The list can drop while you're at lunch or at a kid's game. That's the problem phone alerts solve.

Thursday, Monday and international games

Players in Thursday games lock first, and their inactives arrive Thursday evening. Monday night players give you the most information because you'll see the whole weekend before deciding. International morning games are the easiest to miss. The same 90-minute rule applies to all of them.
