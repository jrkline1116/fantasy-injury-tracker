Fantasy IR Slots vs. the NFL's Injured Reserve

By John Kline · Updated October 16, 2026

Many fantasy leagues include one or more IR slots: roster spots that hold injured players without counting against your bench. Used well, an IR slot is a free extra roster spot. Used carelessly, it can leave you unable to make a waiver claim at the worst moment. The rules aren't the same as the NFL's own injured reserve, and they vary by platform and league.

NFL IR and fantasy IR are different things

The NFL's injured reserve is a roster designation with real rules: a minimum number of games missed and a limit on how many players a team can bring back. (See the designations guide for details.) A fantasy IR slot is simply a place on your fantasy roster, and your league settings decide who's allowed in it. In some leagues only players on the NFL's injured reserve qualify. Many leagues also allow players listed as out, and some include suspended players or the PUP and NFI lists.

Check your league's settings page for the exact eligibility list before you plan around it.

The trap: getting stuck

The common problem comes when a player in your IR slot gets healthier. Once he's no longer eligible (for example, he's activated from the NFL's injured reserve or upgraded from out to questionable), most platforms won't let you make roster moves until you move him back to your active roster or bench. If your bench is full, that means dropping someone before you can claim anyone on waivers.

Plan for it. When a player in your IR slot gets close to returning, decide in advance which bench player you'd cut, so you aren't forced into a bad drop on a Wednesday morning.

Getting the most out of the slot

  • Fill it as soon as someone qualifies. An empty IR slot is a wasted roster spot.
  • Use it for players worth waiting for. A starter expected back for the fantasy playoffs is a great IR stash; a backup who may not have a role when he returns is not.
  • Stash other teams' injured players. If your league allows it, a talented player dropped after an injury can be picked up and stashed on IR for free.
  • Watch for the activation. Teams usually designate a player to return several days or weeks before activating him, which gives you warning to plan the roster move.
